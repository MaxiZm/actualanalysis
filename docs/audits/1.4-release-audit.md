# Method 1.4.0 release audit

The release corrects effort assignment and missing Arena aliases, exposes paired uncertainty for preliminary models, and adds predictive validation. **Neither proposed replacement formula earned promotion.** The published trait structure remains `correlated`, with the existing profile weights and frozen calibration panel.

## Why the original Sol / GPT-5.5 result was misleading

In the published 1.3.2 Chat view, Sol's median was 63.390 and GPT-5.5's was 65.529. Their joint posterior assigned Sol a 40.475% probability of a higher Chat index. The 90% interval for Sol minus GPT-5.5 was −21.420 to +13.235 points. The median ordering did not establish that GPT-5.5 was better.

Sol, Terra and Luna lacked declared reasoning endpoints, so results from different effort levels were pooled into one system. Their named Arena configurations were also missing aliases. GPT-5.5 had correctly separated effort systems, creating an asymmetric comparison. Explicit Arena suffixes now survive alias deduplication and retain their own configuration. Raw score, uncertainty, sample count and observation date are unchanged. See the [source audit](1.4-evidence-audit.md).

The corrected Arena source scores for Sol xhigh and GPT-5.5 high are close: 1482.86794 versus 1482.33430. They are not identical effort configurations or proof of universal superiority. Some matched tests favor GPT-5.5; the release never forces a newer model or a Pro name above another model.

## Formula selection

All candidate comparisons use the same corrected evidence. Complete model × benchmark groups, including every effort setting and report, are excluded from fitting. Held-out cell and family residuals are freshly drawn. Errors are measured on each condition's declared transformed scale, with equal weight per model × benchmark group.

| Evaluation | Baseline group RMSE | Candidate group RMSE | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Shared capability plus domain departures, original held-out test | 0.780787 | 0.794891 | Reject |
| Equal-unit correlated traits plus shared effort gain, all three exploratory folds | 0.610235 | 0.610382 | Retain baseline |

The shared-capability candidate's test RMSE increase was +0.01410, with a model-cluster 90% interval of +0.00073 to +0.02797. CRPS and log predictive density also worsened. Its full-data sampler converged, but numerical convergence did not rescue failed predictive validation.

The equal-unit candidate's aggregate CRPS was 0.312293 versus 0.311415 for baseline; log predictive density was −0.751742 versus −0.745987. The RMSE difference interval, −0.004695 to +0.004737, includes zero. One fold improved slightly and two worsened. There is no demonstrated predictive gain.

The repeated folds contain 262 unique groups across 70 models. Repeated group appearances are averaged before clustering whole models, avoiding fictitious extra independent observations. These are **exploratory repeated cross-validation on an already inspected dataset**, not a new untouched confirmation set. The [validation archive](1.4-validation/README.md) retains all folds, candidate decisions, hashes and reproduction commands.

The corrected fit contains **881 observations, 108 model snapshots, 122 effort systems and 19 benchmark conditions**. All 16 calibration systems remain represented. The likelihood input matches the frozen validation evidence exactly; method metadata and production sampling settings differ.

## Publication safeguards and limits

Production requires four NUTS chains, 2,000 warm-up iterations and 3,000 samples per chain, retaining 8,000 draws. R-hat, effective sample size and score Monte Carlo error now include all three published profiles. E-BFMI uses total Hamiltonian energy. Rejected experimental fits remain in the local database with a rejection reason and cannot enter public snapshots, even through an explicit run-ID request.

Pairwise probabilities come from joint posterior draws and are available for every fitted system, including preliminary estimates. Rank eligibility remains separate. More benchmark coverage is not an explicit score reward; missing observations still affect uncertainty and shrinkage. Domain-specific evidence is uneven, especially for communication, and the retained model's raw-scale effort prior remains a documented limitation.

The release does not establish temporal generalization, robustness to missing-not-at-random reporting, profile-weight optimality, or simulation-based calibration. Artificial Analysis display measurements remain outside the public fit and bulk exports. The default catalog retains all 126 releases; only models with compatible evidence receive fitted estimates.
