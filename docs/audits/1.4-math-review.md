# ACI 1.4 mathematical review

Reviewed 2026-09-05. Baseline: method 1.3.2, accepted local run `work/pipeline/2026-09-04T21-44-30.598Z`, 119 systems, 19 conditions, 573 cells and 889 observations. This review concerns the statistical model and diagnostics; it does not establish that the underlying source records are correct.

## Why the GPT-5.6 Sol / GPT-5.5 Chat comparison fails

The baseline does not support a confident Chat winner. GPT-5.5 has the higher median, but the paired posterior difference is extremely uncertain:

| Baseline quantity | GPT-5.6 Sol | GPT-5.5 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Mixed median | 65.42 | 64.92 |
| Agentic median | 65.12 | 61.18 |
| Chat median | 63.39 | 65.53 |
| Chat 90% interval | 53.54–72.58 | 52.90–81.43 |
| Communication median | 54.99 | 60.55 |
| Communication 90% interval | 36.32–71.04 | 38.71–83.40 |
| Material communication cells | 1 | 1 |

The paired Sol-minus-5.5 Chat median is −2.04 points, with a 90% interval of −21.42 to +13.23. The posterior probability that Sol is higher is 40.5%, and that it is higher by more than one point is 36.0%. Both Chat projections are marked unpublished by the summary's evidence gates, although the UI still exposes provisional estimates.

Communication receives 40% of Chat weight. In this baseline, both systems have only one cell whose communication loading is at least 0.25: LiveBench, itself a mixture of all five traits. A good mixed-test result cannot uniquely identify five abilities. The fit can allocate a nearly equal LiveBench total to stronger reasoning/knowledge/agentic traits and a weaker communication residual. This explains why the inferred communication ordering can contradict the ordering on observed shared tests without providing direct evidence about conversational quality.

This issue is widespread: only 19 of 119 systems have at least two material communication cells. The posterior mean population correlation between software and communication is just 0.042, while agentic and reasoning correlate 0.950. Thus the free covariance model borrows very unevenly across domains.

The mean contributions to the Sol-minus-5.5 Chat difference are code −0.51, reasoning +0.70, knowledge +0.89, and communication −4.00 points. Means are additive; the sum of component medians need not equal the median total.

## A second structural source: effort gains have inconsistent units

The baseline adds a common raw-unit effort mean to every domain, but fitted raw domain spreads differ substantially:

| Domain | Median raw spread | GPT-5.5 max-minus-standard median gain, panel points |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Agentic | 1.270 | 2.57 |
| Software | 0.539 | 5.26 |
| Reasoning | 1.389 | 4.09 |
| Knowledge | 0.956 | 3.81 |
| Communication | 0.322 | 10.04 |

The shared raw effort mean is about 0.291. The same prior increment occupies a much larger fraction of a narrow communication distribution than of a broad reasoning distribution. Sol is designated fixed-effort, so it receives no modeled max-minus-standard increment; GPT-5.5 does. Its communication gain is weakly determined, with a 90% interval of −6.86 to +32.04 panel points.

Within the baseline posterior, GPT-5.5's total max-minus-standard Chat increment has median 9.12 points. The communication contribution alone has median 5.37. This is a decomposition of the existing fit, not a causal estimate of the effect of changing the prior and refitting.

Effort should be parameterized in dimensionless capability units, with a common model-specific gain and smaller, partially pooled domain departures. If raw per-domain spreads remain, multiply the dimensionless increment by those spreads. Fixed-effort systems then receive no extra gain, while flexible systems are assessed in comparable units. Do not impose a deterministic gain or a named-model ordering.

## Identification and model structure

The current documentation's claim of independent per-domain scale invariance is too broad. With fixed cross-loadings,

`eta_sb = beta_b + alpha_b * sum_k(lambda_bk * Z_sk)`,

a transformation `Z'_sk = c_k * Z_sk` can be absorbed into one `alpha'_b` only if `alpha'_b * c_k = alpha_b` for every nonzero loading of that benchmark. Unequal domain scale changes cannot generally be absorbed by one benchmark slope. Domain shifts can be absorbed by benchmark intercepts, and a common scale change can be absorbed by slopes. Separate domain scales alter the meaning of cross-loaded benchmark shares. Normalizing published scores afterwards does not undo that modeling choice.

A defensible candidate is a shared capability factor plus shrunk domain departures in unit marginal-variance coordinates:

`g_m, d_mk ~ Normal(0, 1)`

`h_mk = (g_m + tau_k * d_mk) / sqrt(1 + tau_k^2)`

`tau_k ~ HalfNormal(tau_prior)`.

Conditional on `tau`, each domain has variance one. Correlation between two distinct domains is `1 / sqrt((1 + tau_j^2) * (1 + tau_k^2))`. Therefore sparse domains borrow from the model's common evidence, while sufficiently supported specialization can remain. Taxonomy loadings then operate on comparable marginal units.

This is a structural positive-correlation assumption, not an automatic identification cure or a factual declaration that every capability improves together. With fixed `tau = 0.5`, the implied pairwise correlation is 0.8; with `tau = 1`, it is 0.5. Compare the full fitted candidate against the corrected-data baseline and inspect sensitivity to prior scales 0.25, 0.5 and 1. Avoid choosing a prior because a particular product moves up the table. Preserve intervals and identify extrapolated domains even if shrinkage narrows them.

The existing family and cell terms need predictive scrutiny. There are 18 families for 19 benchmarks, so most family effects have only one benchmark and compete with the system-by-benchmark residual. Their baseline family scale is near zero (median 0.021). There is no evidence from this audit that removing them alone would fix ranking; it is a separate candidate, not a required change.

## Validation required before claiming improvement

Compare old and new structures on identical corrected input. New evidence and a new statistical method must be separated when attributing improvement. A model's own observed-versus-fitted scatter is not evidence of generalization, particularly with a fitted residual per cell.

1. Hold out complete model-by-condition cells, grouping original runs, mirrors and shared lineage. Use model-by-family holdouts as a harder test when versions share tasks. For a missing-condition target, avoid leaking the same result through another effort or version. Preserve sufficient training evidence and condition calibration.
2. Refit. Predict held-out observations without their fitted cell residual or run noise. Draw fresh latent residuals from the learned distribution, and integrate source/protocol uncertainty. For a removed protocol-condition pair, sample its new interaction rather than reading a stale pair index.
3. Evaluate predictive distributions with log predictive score, empirical 90% interval coverage, interval width and errors in meaningful original units. Report by domain, source provenance and coverage level. Macro-average condition/domain results as well as reporting pooled values so dense reasoning rows cannot conceal poor Chat predictions.
4. Add sparse-evidence masking experiments: retain 2, 4 or 8 condition families for well-covered models and predict the remaining cells. This tests the exact coverage problem exposed by this comparison.
5. Assess pairwise ordering on held-out matched tests, including calibration of predicted win probabilities and the fraction of comparisons declared unresolved. A revised median alone is not validation.
6. Reserve chronological or genuinely new-source evidence for a final check. Avoid repeatedly tuning against the same folds until a favored ordering appears.

The held-out unit determines the prediction task. Grouped latent effects make naive pointwise PSIS difficult; exact grouped refits are a clear initial approach. These recommendations follow the distinctions in [Vehtari's cross-validation guidance](https://avehtari.github.io/modelselection/CV-FAQ.html) and the treatment of predictive uncertainty in [Vehtari, Gelman and Gabry](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.04544).

Simulation-based calibration would test inference under a declared generative model; it would not establish that the assumed model describes real benchmarks. The current TypeScript `evaluateSbcMetrics` helper measures supplied bias and interval coverage, not posterior-rank SBC or external validity.

## Diagnostic corrections implemented during this review

The old runner labeled a potential-energy-only ratio as E-BFMI. HMC's total energy includes both potential and kinetic terms; NumPyro exposes them separately in [HMCState](https://num.pyro.ai/en/latest/mcmc.html#numpyro.infer.hmc.HMCState). The runner now collects total `energy`, computes the per-chain ratio using that energy, and treats absent, non-finite or constant energy as unavailable and failing acceptance. Old posterior archives do not contain the missing total-energy sequence, so this audit cannot retroactively validate their E-BFMI.

Agentic and Chat calibrated composites are now monitored explicitly for convergence and display-scale Monte Carlo error, alongside general and domain estimands. The new domain and effort deviation scales are also monitored. Candidate dense-mass adaptation no longer references absent stochastic sites.

Validation helpers now fail empty checks, reject missing or invalid target/interval pairs, reject incomplete comparison refits, and return `rankCoverage: null` when no rank validation was supplied. They no longer report a fabricated constant rank coverage. Five Python diagnostic tests and five TypeScript validation tests exercise these failure modes and the measured calculations.

Five additional Python trait-structure tests check comparable effort units, fixed-effort behavior, invariance to obsolete raw-spread priors, empirical unit marginal variance and correlation, information transfer through the common prior, and support for negative domain specialization favored by direct evidence. These are seeded prior/likelihood checks; they do not replace held-out predictive evaluation.

Reproducible baseline calculations: `work/math-audit/review-baseline.py` and `work/math-audit/review-baseline.json`. No new predictive validation result is claimed by this review.
